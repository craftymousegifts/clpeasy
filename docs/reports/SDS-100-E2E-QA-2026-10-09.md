# CLPeasy® — 100 genuine 10%-in-candle-wax SDS: end-to-end customer-journey QA

**Scope:** for each of 100 genuine Nikura 10%-in-candle-wax supplier SDSs, the exact Section 2.2 text was pasted into the real Smart Paste box of the current production `builder.html` in Chromium. The QA then:

- pressed **Extract hazard data**;
- completed the hazard confirmation and the business details;
- opened the real Step 5 preview at **63 × 44 mm** and **76 × 51 mm**;
- ticked the confirmation and pressed the real **PNG / SVG / PDF** buttons;
- where a size was blocked, followed the builder's own **"Use W×Hmm"** recommendation and exported at that size.

Downloaded files were then checked independently: the file exists, has the correct physical size and content, and matches the preview.

**This is a QA of CLPeasy's existing behaviour, not a regulatory certification.** No production code, settings or data were changed.

## What this means

**8 of 100 cases passed the complete customer journey.** In those cases:
- the supplier's Section 2.2 was extracted correctly;
- the label was correctly blocked at 63 × 44 mm;
- it fitted at 76 × 51 mm;
- the downloaded PNG, SVG and PDF matched the preview and the supplier's label elements.

**Size safety works.** In every case that reached Step 5, a label too full for its size was not exported:
- the existing "Your label needs more space" warning was shown;
- the preview read "FULL CONTENT DOES NOT FIT";
- the download buttons were disabled, a forced click was refused, and no download was charged;
- the builder's "Use W×Hmm" recommendation always produced a label that fits.

No case exported an overfull label, and no text silently disappeared. All 183 downloaded files (61 exported label sizes × PNG, SVG and PDF) had the correct physical size (PNG at 600 dpi; SVG and PDF in mm) and matched the preview.

**The main problems are in Smart Paste extraction, and they carry through to the printed label.**

1. **D1/D2 — EUH208 sensitiser names (46 cases).** Smart Paste drops, rewrites or merges the supplier's named sensitisers, or picks up PDF layout text. For example:
   - **011:** the supplier lists *Terpinolene, d-Limonene, dl-Limonene, p-Mentha-1,3-diene*. The label says *Contains: Limonene*.
   - **010, 014:** no names are extracted at all. In 014 the builder correctly refuses to export ("EUH208 needs the named sensitising substance(s)"), so the customer cannot finish.
   - **015, 039 and 19 others:** "Information:" from the PDF's left-hand column ends up inside a chemical name. In **039** the downloaded PDF prints *"…l-.β.-Bisabolene. Information:"*.
   - **32 cases** reached a downloaded label, and the wrong names are printed on it (D4).
2. **D3 — valid precautionary statements rejected (6 cases: 004, 005, 053, 095, 097, 100).** The builder says "CLP code not recognised" for **P202, P308+P313 and P310** and stops the customer at Step 3. These statements appear in the CLP Annex IV list retained in GB CLP. The renderer's P-statement library does not include them.
3. **D5 — pictogram precedence (097).** The supplier's H315 + H318 + H361 needs GHS05 + GHS08, and the supplier shows only those two. The builder also selects GHS07. CLP Art. 26(1)(c) says GHS07 should not appear for skin or eye irritation when GHS05 applies. It isn't printed here only because 097 is already stopped by D3.

**Printed precautionary statements and text size (checked on all 61 downloaded label sizes).**
- **Every P statement selected in the builder is printed** on the label: 287 statement placements, none missing.
- **R4 (11 cases: 021, 046, 054, 061, 067, 084, 085, 087, 088, 089, 092):** P305+P351+P338 is printed only as *"IF IN EYES: rinse cautiously with water for several minutes"*. *"Remove contact lenses, if present and easy to do. Continue rinsing."* from the supplier's (and the CLP Annex IV) wording is left out. This comes from the renderer's own statement wording, so it needs owner review.
- **Smallest printed text:** 1.20–1.32 mm font size (about 0.62–0.68 mm x-height in DM Sans) on every downloaded label. That is never below CLPeasy's own GB floor of 1.2 mm (`GB_ACTIVE_MIN_FS_MM`). GB CLP itself sets no numeric minimum, so this confirms the builder keeps to its own rule. It is not a legal judgement.

**Supplier-document issues (S1, 24 cases).** Nikura's Section 2.2 uses GHS-only codes (H401, H402, H316) that are not part of GB CLP. The builder correctly stops and asks for supplier clarification. 14 cases have no other issue and are SUPPLIER REVIEW; 10 also have a CLPeasy defect and are counted under FAIL.

**Owner decisions needed:**
- **R1 (81 cases):** CLPeasy deliberately leaves P272/P280/P363/P405 (and P264/P362) off consumer labels. The production builder shows the customer **no notice** that supplier statements were omitted; there is no notice element.
- **R2 (7 cases: 001, 006, 009, 012, 042, 063, 064):** the supplier SDS says the mixture is unclassified ("Signal word: None / Hazard statements: None"). The builder will not let the customer continue ("Please extract hazard data…"), so no label, including the EN 15494 candle pictograms, can be produced.

## Method and environment

- **Inputs:** the 100 PDFs and exact Section 2.2 texts from workflow run 37906750853. All 100 PDF hashes and all 100 text hashes match `manifest.json`, so no collection was repeated. Each case's `section-2-2-pasted.txt` is the exact text pasted, and `source-section-2-2.png` is the section rendered from the supplier's own PDF.
- **Builder under test:** `builder.html` on `qa/smart-paste-sds-corpus`, which is **byte-identical to current `main` (85af515)**. The live site serves the same file apart from Netlify's pretty-URL link rewriting. No product code was changed.
- **Customer actions, in headless Chromium:**
  1. Step 1: chose Rectangle and typed W and H.
  2. Step 2: entered product name, type *Scented Candle* and fragrance load *10%*.
  3. Step 3: **pasted** the text (`Input.insertText`, the same path as a clipboard paste), pressed **Extract hazard data**, ticked the hazard confirmation and pressed **Next**.
  4. Step 4: entered business details.
  5. Step 5: ticked the confirmation, then pressed the **PNG / SVG / PDF** buttons.
  - Each size ran in a fresh browser profile, so cases cannot contaminate each other. **Clear hazard data** was also exercised after every run, and nothing stale remained.
- **Product and business details** are clearly fictional test fixtures and are not part of Section 2.2: "Fernhill QA Candles", "1 Test Lane, Exampleton, EX1 2AB", "01632 960000" (an Ofcom drama number). No supplier facts were invented.
- **Downloads used an offline entitlement fixture.** The Supabase client was replaced in the browser by a local stand-in for a signed-in Pay As You Go customer with purchased downloads, so the real export code ran. **No request reached production Supabase, Stripe, any customer account or payment.** All non-local requests were answered locally, except Google Fonts (the label typeface a real customer loads).
- **PDF export:** the builder opens its print view, and that view was saved with Chromium's own Save as PDF, as a customer would.
- **Independent checks** (`scripts/e2e-sds-analyse.py`):
  - The supplier's label elements were derived from the supplier text, not the builder: H/EUH/P codes, EUH208 names, signal word, and the pictograms required by those H codes (CLP Annex V, Art. 26 precedence).
  - These were compared with the populated builder, the rendered preview SVG and the downloaded files.
  - Files were checked for PNG pixels at 600 dpi, SVG/PDF mm size, PDF text and a PDF-render-vs-PNG visual difference.
  - This independent derivation reproduced all 46 historical extraction FAILs.

## Observations that are not product defects

- The **PNG export** draws label text in a fallback sans-serif font, because a web font can't load inside the SVG-to-image step. SVG and PDF use DM Sans. Content and layout match: PDF-render vs PNG mean pixel difference is at most 9.95/255 across all 61 export sets.
- The **PNG has no DPI metadata.** Pixel size is exact for 600 dpi, but some print dialogs may assume 72/96 dpi.
- **Not loaded in QA:** Plausible analytics and the JSZip CDN (used only by batch zips). Neither affects single-label export.

## Limits of this QA

- **38 cases** stop at Step 3 (D3, S1, R2), so their size and export stages could not be reached without altering the supplier's codes, which the builder itself rightly forbids. They are recorded per size as "BLOCKED (journey stopped before preview)".
- **Only 12 cases fit at 76 × 51 mm** and no case fits at 63 × 44 mm. All other exports were taken at the builder's own recommended size.
- Exports were produced as a simulated paying customer. Trial watermarking was not part of this run.
- This QA compares CLPeasy with the supplier's own Section 2.2. It is not a regulatory certification of the finished labels.
