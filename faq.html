Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about CLP labels, CLPeasy, and staying compliant as a UK maker.

Getting Started
What is a CLP label and do I need one? ▼

CLP stands for Classification, Labelling and Packaging. Under GB CLP Regulation (the UK's retained version of EU CLP), you must apply a hazard label to any product you supply that contains substances classified as hazardous — including fragrance oils above certain concentration thresholds.

For most candle, wax melt, and reed diffuser makers using fragrance oils, a CLP label is a legal requirement before you can sell your products.

What products does CLPeasy support? ▼

CLPeasy supports CLP label generation for fragranced products including candles, wax melts, reed diffusers, room sprays, and other fragrance or essential oil-based products where a CLP hazard label is required.

Bath bombs, soaps & cosmetic products: These products may require a CLP hazard label and a separate cosmetic product label under UK Cosmetics Regulation. CLPeasy covers the CLP label requirement only. See the question below for more detail.

Who is the "responsible person" under GB CLP? ▼

Under GB CLP Regulation, the responsible person is the manufacturer, importer, or downstream user who places the product on the market. As a maker selling your candles or wax melts, that is you.

CLPeasy assists you in generating your CLP label — but you remain legally responsible for ensuring the hazard information on your label is accurate, up to date, and reflects your actual product at your actual fragrance load percentage. Always verify against your supplier's current SDS.

I make bath bombs and soaps — do I need a CLP label? ▼

Possibly yes — and there is an important distinction to understand here.

Bath bombs and soaps can require a CLP hazard label if the fragrance components in your recipe meet classification thresholds under GB CLP Regulation. This is separate to the cosmetic product label.

Two separate requirements may apply:

1. CLP hazard label — required if fragrance components meet classification thresholds. CLPeasy covers this.

2. Cosmetic product label — governed by UK Cosmetics Regulation. Requires a Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR) completed by a qualified cosmetic safety assessor, plus an INCI ingredient list, responsible person details, batch number, and other mandatory elements. CLPeasy does not cover this.

If you are unsure, consult the OPSS guidance on cosmetic products or speak with a qualified cosmetic safety assessor.

Does CLPeasy classify my fragrance for me? ▼

No — and this is an important distinction. CLPeasy does not independently classify substances. It assists you in applying the hazard classifications already provided by your fragrance supplier in their Safety Data Sheet (Section 2.2).

Your fragrance supplier is responsible for classifying their fragrance oil. Your SDS contains that classification data. CLPeasy takes that data and renders the full written hazard statements and precautionary statements on your label — not codes alone — formatted correctly at your specific fragrance load percentage.

What is a UFI code and do I need one? ▼

A UFI (Unique Formula Identifier) is a 16-character code that links your product label to the formula information submitted to the GB Poison Centre Notification portal. For products classified as hazardous mixtures supplied to consumers or professional users in Great Britain, UFI notification is required.

CLPeasy includes a UFI code field on your label. You generate your UFI via the ECHA UFI generator and submit your product details via the GB PCN portal. Your CLPeasy label includes the correct UFI format (UFI: XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX).

Using CLPeasy
Where do I find the hazard classification data for my fragrance? ▼

Your fragrance supplier is required to provide a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for every fragrance oil they sell. The hazard classification information you need is in Section 2.2 of that SDS — it lists the hazard statements (H-statements), precautionary statements (P-statements), signal word (Warning or Danger), and GHS pictograms.

If your supplier does not provide an SDS, ask for one — they are legally required to supply it. CLPeasy's Smart Paste feature lets you paste your SDS Section 2.2 text and extracts the classification data automatically, rendering the full written statements on your label.

Can I save and reuse my labels? ▼

Yes. CLPeasy lets you save your safety sheets to your library and reload them any time. This means once you have set up a product, generating a new label or reprinting an existing one takes seconds.

We recommend reviewing your saved labels whenever your fragrance supplier updates their SDS, as hazard classifications can change between batches or supplier reformulations.
